Fair Share: style directions

Phase 3 design mockups, round 2. D and E follow the feedback on the first round: start from the look of the previous Fair Share prototype, make the charts the star, and draw clear lines between the inputs, the results and the data tables. Both show the same content: header and nav, an input panel, the $60,000 example result, the people vs income bars with a top 1% inset, the stretched-percentile chart, data table toggles and a short text block. Each follows your system light or dark setting and has a theme button (Auto, Light, Dark) in the header.

D. Studio

The previous prototype, refined. A cool slate page with white rounded cards, Inter throughout and an indigo accent, with the inputs in a tinted indigo panel on the left (it stays in view as you scroll on wide screens) and results stacked on the right. The result card keeps the old layout: a big difference number with a colored top edge beside a recessed comparison box. Tables open into a grey recessed well under each chart, so they never blend into the chart or the inputs. The closest to what Fair Share looked like before.

Open D. Studio

E. Harbor

Same card system with more contrast between areas. The inputs sit in one deep teal control band across the top (three columns on wide screens), so "what you set" is visually separate from "what you get". Below it, a white result card with a teal edge next to a tinted text card, then full-width chart cards so the charts get the most room. A warm stone page, Plus Jakarta Sans, teal accent, and dashed-edge table wells. Less like an app, a little more like a magazine feature.

Earlier rounds

The first three directions, kept for reference. These have no input panel.

A. Broadsheet

A warm newsprint page in a single centered column, like a long-read in a print paper. Newsreader serif headlines and an italic dek over Source Sans 3 body, hairline and double rules, a brick-red accent, and a cool-to-warm group scale (blues for the bottom 90%, ochre to deep red for the top). Calm and traditional, with the most reading-first feel of the three.

Open A. Broadsheet

B. Ledger

A crisp, cool, slightly technical page that looks at home in dark mode. Spectral headlines, IBM Plex Sans body and Plex Mono for every number. On wide screens the result card sits beside the text like a side note and charts run full width in panels. A teal accent and a sequential blue-to-magenta group scale. The densest and most "tool-like" of the three while staying editorial.

Open B. Ledger

C. Poster

A bold, bright explainer in the spirit of The Pudding. DM Serif Display headlines at very large sizes over DM Sans, full-width colored section bands, thick outlines and offset shadows, and oversized numbers in the result card. An electric blue accent and a distinct categorical group palette. The most playful and attention-grabbing, best suited to the scrolling story.

Open C. Poster
